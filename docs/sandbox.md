# The sandbox

Try a function out without anything happening in your home. Back to the [README](../README.md) or the [guide](guide.md).

**Sandbox** (in the function's toolbar) tries the function out on a copy of its states. Each time it opens, a dialog explains what it's for and everything it can't show you, to click through. Set a state or an attribute, press a device's button, fire an event, call a webhook or run an automation (every field a list of what fits the function, with Other… for anything else), and the board shows what the function would do: what triggers, which conditions pass and why, what each action changes, and what that sets off next, lit up on the board and listed in the drawer. Nothing is sent to your devices, and the board goes back to your home's states when you close it.

Next moved its clock to the timer finishing: "Garage: left open" ran, auto-close was on, "Close garage" closed the door and the timer was cancelled, sixteen numbered steps on the board and in the drawer, with ◀ ▶ Replay and Clear under the board](images/sandbox.png)

- **Its controls in sections.** The drawer's controls are grouped under five headers: **Change something** (Set, Press, Z-Wave, MQTT), **Send an event** (an event, a notification's button, a webhook, a tag, a calendar, Assist, Home Assistant starting or stopping), **Run and replay** (Run, What if, Replay), **Time** (the clock, Jump to) and **Settings** (how a device answers, Whole home, Step through). A section only shows when the function has something for it, and clicking its header folds it away; folded sections stay folded next time, for you only.
- **What to set, with the values that matter.** The Set list starts with every entity the automations read (triggers, waits and conditions, including ones reached through an area or a device), then the rest of the board. Its values say why: for "CO2 above 1100" they're 1101 (crosses it) and 1100 (doesn't), for a zone trigger the zones by name, for an event entity a press of each type the trigger waits for. An attribute a trigger reads (a light's brightness, a thermostat's hvac_action) can be picked beside the state.
- **Its own clock.** Delays, timers, `for:` durations, time and sun triggers and time patterns run on the sandbox clock, shown with the day and the time to the second. **Advance by** 1 second to a week (or type +90 min), **Jump to** what's due, sunrise, sunset, a time trigger, the edge of a time condition, midnight, or a time you type (21:30, 2026-12-24 07:00), or **Next**. Each thing coming up is a button that runs the clock to it. "Left open for 15 minutes, then close it" takes a click.
- **Templates are real.** Home Assistant renders each template the way it would in a real run, but `states()`, `is_state()`, `state_attr()`, `states.x.y`, `states[...]`, `states | ...`, `expand()`, `now()` and the like read the sandbox's states and clock. A template that reads locations (`closest()`, `distance()`) is marked *partly real*.
- **A debugger.** The drawer has four tabs. **Timeline** puts a sentence over each thing you did ("Motion turned on: Hall light on, timer started") and shows a run's variables (`trigger`, `repeat`, `wait`, responses) at any line you click; **Undo** takes back your last step and **Again** repeats it. **States** lists every entity with its state and attributes, the changed ones first, each editable in place. **Explore** tries everything the function can be given, one thing at a time from the same start, and flags what never happens, conditions that are never true, two automations fighting over one entity, and loops. **Coverage** marks on the board what ran and what didn't. **Step** makes every run wait before each condition and action until you press Continue, and **Pause here** on a box's bar does it for one automation.
- **How devices answer, and what if.** By default a device does what it's told at once; the **Device** row makes one slow (a cover is opening first), fail (a lock jams) or go unavailable. **What if** has ready-made situations: everyone leaves or comes home, a power cut and power back, sunset, sunrise, the night, a morning, a day, a week. The clock can also run by itself, from real time to 600 times faster.
- **Helpers that follow what they read.** Template sensors and binary sensors, min/max sensors, threshold sensors and binary sensor, light and switch groups follow their inputs as Home Assistant's own do (the same rules, rounding and hysteresis), before any automation hears the change. Other helpers worked out from something (a derivative, a utility meter, statistics) stay as they are unless you set them.
- **Replay from history.** **Replay** (last night, or the last 6, 12 or 24 hours) starts the sandbox from your home as it was then, from Home Assistant's own history, and gives it the real changes of everything the function reads, in order, with the clock. Then it shows how that compares with what really happened: when each of its automations ran in your home and here, and what the function changed on its own. Anything that differs is listed with when. The replay can be saved as a check like anything else you do. It only reads the history (it needs the recorder and history integrations, which Home Assistant has by default).
- **Notification buttons, Zigbee2MQTT and Frigate.** The **Notification** row presses any button the function's notifications have, firing the event the phone's app would. MQTT suggestions include a Zigbee2MQTT button's action as the JSON it sends, and the events, counts and motion Frigate publishes.
- **Whole home.** Ticked in the intro or the Home row, the sandbox also runs the automations outside the function that start from what it reads or changes, and the ones those reach in turn (up to 200), so you see what else in your home a change sets off. They show beside the board as not on it, are left out of the function's coverage, and a check saved this way replays them too.
- **The same order as Home Assistant.** When one change starts several runs, they go as they would in Home Assistant: conditions are checked before a mode looks at the runs already going, each run goes as far as it can at once before the next trigger is looked at, and a change made meanwhile waits its turn. A test runs the same homes in the sandbox and in Home Assistant's own core and compares them step by step, so the two stay the same.
- **It asks what it can't know**: an integration's own condition, or a template Home Assistant couldn't render. Your answer is used for that step.
- **What it follows:** state, numeric state, event, MQTT, time, time pattern, template, zone and sun triggers (with `for:` and offsets), device triggers on an entity (followed as the state trigger they stand for), and Home Assistant's per-entity triggers (`light.turned_on`, `door.opened`, `lock.unlocked`, `event.received`, thresholds and the rest), by the same rules as Home Assistant's own; state, numeric state, template, time, trigger, zone, sun, device (on an entity) and/or/not conditions; service calls on helpers, lights, switches, covers, locks, climate, timers, counters, selects and more, and `mqtt.publish`; scripts, `choose`, `if`, `repeat`, `parallel`, `variables`, `wait_template`, `wait_for_trigger` (any of those triggers), `stop`, and the run modes (single, restart, queued, parallel). Notifications, TTS and log messages are listed as messages; any other call it doesn't know the effect of is listed as one it would make. A change that sets off more than 2,000 steps is stopped as a loop.
- **What you do yourself**, each a row only when the function has one: **Press** a device's own button or remote (ZHA, Z-Wave, Hue, deCONZ, MQTT device triggers), or any other trigger the sandbox can't see coming (Z-Wave value updated, geo location), fired as it's configured. Press lists every trigger of the function's devices from Home Assistant's own list, not only those its automations use, with the choices Home Assistant offers for each (a scene button pressed, held or released): a press starts every trigger it satisfies, so one set to held doesn't start on a press. An MQTT device's trigger is sent as the message it stands for (its topic and payload), so everything on that topic hears it too: an event entity on the same topic, or an MQTT trigger; **Z-Wave** reports a value of the function's Z-Wave devices (a lock's mode, a door's state, a battery level), picked from the states it can take: Home Assistant's `zwave_js.value_updated` triggers on it start (their From and To read as Home Assistant reads them), and the entities that show it follow (a lock, a binary sensor, a sensor, a switch). A scene button pressed is the `zwave_js_value_notification` event Home Assistant fires, which event triggers and the scene's event entity hear too; **MQTT** sends a message on a topic the function listens to; **Webhook**, **Tag**, **Calendar** (an event starts or ends), **Assist** (a sentence, matched against the trigger's templates) and **HA** (Home Assistant starts or shuts down). **Run** an automation as if any one of its triggers fired, so `trigger.id` conditions and `trigger.` templates work, or a script with its fields. Everything you do is kept in a saved check.
- Only the automations and scripts on the board run in the sandbox, unless Whole home is ticked. Test buttons and the Test dialog act on the sandbox while it's open.
- **Follow it on the board.** Whatever you do is played on the board step by step: each step's arrow lights with a dot running along it and a number beside it, conditions show ✓ or ✕ on their arrows, and what isn't on the board (the event a run stood in for, a sensor it checked, the MQTT topic a script publishes to) gets a temporary dashed box beside what it touches. A step that reaches a device's entities, or an area's lights, says over that box what it did: the action, which of them, and with what. A state trigger with From and no To that fires on a change into a state it lists under From (`from: [off, on]` fires both ways) is pointed out in the timeline. A box with a delay running shows how long is left. Step with ◀ ▶ in the bar under the board, or click a row in the drawer.

**Saved checks.** After doing something in the sandbox, **Save as a check** keeps the steps, the states and the time they started from, and what came of it (you tick which: the door ends closed, "Close garage" runs to the end, an automation is stopped by its conditions or doesn't run). The board replays every check on the function's current configs when they change and when you open it; a check that stops passing is a red problem saying what happened instead, and **Replay** plays it in the sandbox, step by step. It's a regression test for a part of your home, and like the sandbox it never touches your devices. **Edit** changes a check's name, the order and values of its steps and what it expects; **Share** gives it as text to copy, and **Import** (beside Save as a check) adds one someone pasted, keeping only what a check can hold. A shared check names the entities it uses and the states it starts from, so share it only where those names are fine to show.
